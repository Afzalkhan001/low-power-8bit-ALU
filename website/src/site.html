Design and simulation of a low-power 8-bit ALU

An arithmetic logic unit is the part of a processor that does the math. This one adds, subtracts, multiplies, compares, shifts and runs logic operations on two 8-bit numbers. It is built from 3,738 transistors, and idle blocks are switched off at their inputs to save power.

Abstract

This project designs an 8-bit arithmetic logic unit (ALU) at transistor level in CMOS and simulates it with Cadence Spectre using the 45 nm Generic PDK (gpdk045). The ALU takes two 8-bit operands, A and B, and a 4-bit opcode. It performs 16 operations: addition, subtraction, increment, decrement, 8×8 multiplication with a 16-bit product, comparison, seven bitwise logic operations, two shifts and a rotate. It produces carry, zero, negative and overflow flags.

The datapath uses 28-transistor mirror full adders in a ripple-carry chain, an array multiplier, and transmission-gate multiplexers. To lower power, the design uses operand isolation: the opcode decoder enables one functional block at a time, and AND gates hold the inputs of the idle blocks at zero so they do not switch. Other techniques are minimum-size devices, gated control signals and supply-voltage scaling. Each block has its own supply rail so its power can be measured separately.

The circuit was checked exhaustively at gate level (16 operations × 65,536 operand pairs) and at switch level on the full transistor netlist. A browser-based virtual lab shows every internal bit, how signals propagate, and the estimated energy saved by isolation.

Objectives

  1. Design a 16-operation, 8-bit ALU from CMOS transistors.
  2. Lower dynamic power with operand isolation, small devices and voltage scaling.
  3. Simulate it in Cadence Spectre and check every operation against a golden model.
  4. Measure delay, average power per block and energy per operation.
  5. Compare the design with isolation on and off, and at 1.0 V and 0.8 V.
